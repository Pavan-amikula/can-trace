From CAN traffic to similarity

Four successive messages of the same ID form 88 sites: 11 identifier bits, 8 payload bytes, DLC, bus gap and same-ID gap per message. Each site maps to [cos(0.9x), sin(0.9x)]. Each arbitration ID has its own normal-only sum of product states, compressed through QR/SVD sweeps to a maximum bond dimension of 4.

The raw score is −log of the absolute overlap with the matching ID prototype, clipped to [10⁻¹⁵, 1]. It is divided by that ID’s 99th-percentile benign-calibration score; a ratio above 1 alerts. Unknown IDs receive an explicit novelty score of 2. This is a similarity score, not an attack probability or a normalized Born likelihood.

Bond entropy diagnostic

Schmidt entropies of the first ID prototype, in bits. These summarize dependencies retained by the representation; they do not establish physical CAN signal causality.

Known boundaries

Short context can miss stealthy replay and long-range dynamics. Byte padding and cold-start zero gaps are explicit representation choices. The detector supports classical 11-bit CAN, one channel at a time. No DBC semantics, ECU identity, CAN FD or safety certification is inferred.

What this prototype demonstrates

Normal-only learning, actual MPS compression, sequence scoring, feature sensitivity, baseline evaluation, replay and content-addressed evidence exports.

What remains open

Cross-vehicle tests, unseen attack families, long-context replay, robust event-level thresholds, DBC-aware explanations, ECU deployment and acquisition authentication.

Forensic relevance

Source/model hashes and reproducible configuration support technical traceability. This project does not claim compliance with UNECE R155 or ISO/SAE 21434.
